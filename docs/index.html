Your coin launched. Now what?

Most tokens have nothing to do after launch. No reason to hold, nothing burning supply, and the creator fees just sit in a wallet. Arena gives any token a game — a live pot fed by its own trading fees, and a slot people burn its supply to take.

How it works

1Point it at your mint

Paste your token's address, pick the round length and your cut. One transaction. No approval, no waiting, no gatekeeper.

2Send your fees in

Creator fees go into your game's pot instead of sitting idle. Anyone can top it up — that instruction is permissionless.

3Holders play

Buy the last slot by burning your token. Every buy resets the clock and shortens it. Last one standing at zero takes the pot.

4Supply falls

Every buy destroys tokens against your mint. Not moved to a wallet — burned. Your supply only goes one way.

What is impossible, rather than promised

We cannot touch your pot

The platform account is not even in the settle instruction. It is structurally impossible for us to take, block or redirect a payout. Not a policy — check the source.

Fees are capped in code

Platform share can never exceed 10%, registration never above 5 SOL, your own cut never above 20%. The program rejects anything higher. We could not raise them later if we wanted to.

Your settings freeze at registration

Timer, price floor, step, both cuts — fixed when you attach. Nobody can tilt a running game, including you.

Payout needs nobody's permission

Once a clock hits zero anyone can trigger the payout, and it always pays the last buyer rather than whoever pressed the button. If our keeper stops, your players still get paid.

Where this actually is

The program is written and 21 tests pass on a local validator — including that two games cannot touch each other's pots, that you cannot play one game with another token, and that the platform authority has no route to any pot.

It is not audited and not deployed to mainnet. It will hold money for every token that attaches, so it needs a proper audit before it holds anyone's. Saying so here rather than burying it.
